01 / PRIMATES · SOCIAL COGNITION

Primate social cognition and theory of mind

What have others seen, and what do they know? We study primate gaze, action prediction and interactions to understand cognition in social life, connecting individual cognition with group behaviour through experimental tasks and natural social settings.

Ongoing pilot research

Primate group behavior and decision-making

How do monkeys use the movements and choices of others when deciding what to do? We study feeding-site choices within groups and use cameras and automated measurement to investigate decisions by individuals and collectives.

02 / HUMANS · DEVELOPMENT

Research planning and task development

Understanding others through anticipatory looking in adults and children

Animated scenarios let us study where people look as they anticipate a character’s next action, based on what that character has seen or believes. Building on task development with adults, we plan to extend this approach to children and investigate social cognition beyond verbal answers.

ALFB tasks examine anticipatory looking when a character acts on a false belief. The film is an example animated stimulus for human research, not footage of participants or a presentation of study results.

03 / COMPUTER VISION · NATURAL BEHAVIOUR

Ongoing methods development

Measuring natural behaviour with computer vision

We quantify individual positions, postures and behaviours from video in natural environments and groups. We are developing 3D reconstruction and AI-based video analysis in connection with our primate research.

YOLO-Behaviour is a published framework for automatically classifying and quantifying animal behaviour from video. Its supplementary film demonstrates the method across study systems; it is not footage of results from our current Kyushu primate project.

04 / EYE COLOUR · GAZE COMMUNICATION

Ongoing research and hypothesis development

Eye colour, white sclera and the evolution of communication

How do eye colour and the white of the eye shape gaze communication in humans and other primates? Through comparative research and behavioural experiments, we examine effects of viewing conditions, including light and distance, and evaluate hypotheses about the evolution of gaze signalling.

The 2022 image-based experiments and the 2026 live-model study examine different viewing conditions. White sclera should not be assumed to offer the same advantage in every context.

Published research

Crow gaze and social cognition

To study crow attention and social cognition, we combine motion capture of freely moving large-billed crows with measurements of their visual fields. Published studies show how visual-field use changes between moving and stationary targets, building a quantitative foundation for investigating what birds see.

Gaze is inferred from head orientation and visual-field anatomy. This is distinct from directly measuring eye movements; these findings do not establish theory of mind in crows.
